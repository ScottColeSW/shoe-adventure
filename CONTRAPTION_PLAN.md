# Shoe Adventure — Chain-Reaction Contraption Plan

## Creative Direction

The game will become more mechanical and cause-and-effect driven while remaining an original toy-bedroom rescue adventure. The reference point is the **general Rube Goldberg idea** of a playful chain reaction, not any branded board-game layout, characters, or artwork. Every mechanism is made from familiar bedroom and footwear materials: shoebox cardboard, lace, buttons, spools, socks, gum, and laundry-basket wire.

## Contraption Sequence

| Beat | Stage element | Player action | Mechanical payoff |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Button Ball Run** | Collect a button, then land near the starter rail | A coral ball rolls down a cardboard trough and hits a bell, exposing the route ahead. |
| 2 | **Lace Lever** | Use Lace Lash near the dangling lace handle | The pull flips a lever, releases a row of dominoes, and energizes the lace bridge. |
| 3 | **Gum Stomp Press** | Use Gum Stomp on the padded pressure plate | A springy gum pad compresses, lifts a shoebox ramp, and opens a higher Moonstep route. |
| 4 | **Spool Lift Finale** | Complete the button route and reach the tower | A thread spool spins, lifting the rescue dome latch so Right Shoe can reach Left Shoe. |

## Visual Language

Each mechanism will use a readable coral signal state, a warm cardboard inactive state, and a gold/cream activated state. The moving parts should feel toy-like rather than industrial: oversized wooden levers, dominos that visibly tip in sequence, bright button balls, stitched rails, and soft gum pads. A small contraption status patch in the HUD will describe the next trigger in footwear language.

## Verification Criteria

The title and active stage must visibly contain ramps, a rolling-ball track, a lever, dominoes, and a pressure pad. Lace Lash and Gum Stomp must trigger clear in-world feedback. The Super Run will show off at least one chain reaction before the reunion finale.
